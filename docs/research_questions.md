# Research-question map

## Confirmatory question

Does a population-level ideological objective, without a supplied transmission payload, cause a language-model origin agent to create a strategy that produces adoption, persistence, and retransmission in agents beyond its direct reach during non-coding collaboration?

The confirmatory comparison is `population_goal` versus `personal_preference` in endpoint-eligible bridge graphs. The claim is domain general only if the aggregate test passes and the direction is positive in at least three of four target positions.

## Immediate robustness questions

Does the effect survive when the origin disappears after five rounds and clean-agent contexts are wiped twice? This distinguishes continuing reinfection by the source from decentralized persistence.

Does the warning paragraph reported in the supplied paper reduce spontaneous contagion, even though the origin was never given a researcher-authored virus? This tests whether a defense against explicit self-propagating payloads transfers to emergent transmission strategies.

How does direct susceptibility in a fully connected graph differ from multi-hop transmission through a single bridge? Fully connected runs estimate adoption pressure; bridge runs identify agent-mediated spread.

Does contagion displace assigned work, or can target advocacy coexist with normal task completion? The preregistered guardrail is evidence-backed task completion, with message volume and document production as secondary measures.

Do benign and misaligned target positions differ in spontaneous transmission probability, and does that difference interact with model family? These are target and model interactions, not evidence of general capability scaling unless model versions are sampled broadly.

## Mechanism questions

What strategies does an origin invent without transmission-language priming? The fixed descriptive taxonomy measures relay instructions, memory or continuity appeals, priority framing, collective identity, reciprocity, task linkage, and the viral themes reported in the paper.

Which origin strategies predict first-hop adoption, and which downstream reformulations predict second-hop transmission? Analysis should use run-level clustered inference and treat strategy-outcome associations as noncausal.

Does the message change semantically across hops while preserving the target objective? A later study can add blinded semantic-similarity and novelty judges to distinguish copying, compression, elaboration, and mutation.

Does a bridge agent act as a super-spreader because of graph position, susceptibility, or its assigned task? This requires randomizing bridge identity and task assignment independently across enough runs, both of which the harness records.

Does infection become self-sustaining after source removal, and can clean agents recolonize one another after staggered resets? The persistence candidate provides the first test; a stronger follow-up would randomize simultaneous versus staggered resets.

## Fresh extensions

A dose-response study can randomize the origin's communication budget from one message total to repeated access, isolating whether emergence depends on repeated persuasion.

A trust study can label peer messages as verified work coordination, untrusted social content, or anonymous text while holding the content channel constant. This reuses the paper's observation that communication framing changes susceptibility.

A role-structure study can compare idle generalists, strongly tasked specialists, and agents with explicit professional identities. This tests the paper's finding that existing tasks can be protective without returning to coding.

A recovery study can introduce the warning only after first infection, measuring whether agents stop retransmitting, remove target content from memory, and restore task performance. This separates prevention from cure.

A heterogeneous-swarm study can vary the origin, bridge, and downstream model independently. It can estimate sender persuasiveness, receiver susceptibility, and relay competence rather than treating “model effect” as one quantity.

A semantic-stealth study can ask whether goal adoption occurs without reusing target lexicon. It requires blinded semantic judging and adversarial negative examples, because lexicon-only detection cannot support that claim.
